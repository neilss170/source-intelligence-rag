# Northwind Logistics: 2025 Operations Report

## Overview

Northwind Logistics closed 2025 with revenue of 48.2 million EUR, an increase of 9% on the previous year, driven mostly by new pharmaceutical contracts in Switzerland and the Benelux and by a steady recovery in retail volumes during the second half of the year. The company now operates a fleet of 214 trucks and six warehouses across five countries, and handled just over 1.3 million pallet movements during the year. Operating margin improved slightly, from 6.1% to 6.6%, although the gain was smaller than planned because insurance and labour costs rose faster than expected. The board considers the year a solid one but has flagged that growth in 2026 will depend on two things going right at the same time: the warehouse software migration has to land without disrupting customers, and the company has to find enough drivers to staff the routes it has already sold. Both of those topics are covered in detail later in this report, together with the investment plan that the board approved in December.

## Warehouses

Rotterdam remains the largest site in the network at 41,000 square metres, and it handles roughly a third of all pallet movements, including almost all of the temperature-controlled pharmaceutical volume. The Lyon warehouse opened in April 2025 after an eight-month fit-out and reached its planned throughput in September, which was two months later than the original plan because racking deliveries slipped. The Gdańsk site will close during 2026 because its lease expires and the landlord has declined to renew it on acceptable terms; its customers will be moved to the Poznań site, which has spare capacity after a reorganisation of its mezzanine floor. Across the network, the average dock-to-stock time, measured from the moment a truck is unloaded to the moment its goods are available for picking, fell to 3.4 hours from 4.1 hours a year earlier. Most of that improvement came from Rotterdam, where inbound appointments are now booked through a self-service portal rather than by phone, which smoothed the arrival of trucks across the day and cut the morning queues that used to build up at the gate.

## Fleet and fuel

During the year 38 electric vans joined the fleet, all of them deployed on last-mile routes in Rotterdam, Antwerp and Lyon, where city centre access rules increasingly favour zero-emission vehicles. Diesel spending fell by 11% compared with 2024, partly because of the electric vans and partly because of a route optimisation project that reduced empty kilometres. The fleet is managed by Tomasz Wiśniewski, who joined from a parcel carrier in 2023 and leads a team of eleven planners and workshop staff. A pilot of HVO biofuel, a renewable diesel made from waste oils, is running on twelve heavy trucks based in Antwerp; early results show no measurable loss of performance and a reduction in reported well-to-wheel emissions of around 80% for those vehicles, although the fuel costs about 18% more per litre than standard diesel. A decision on extending the pilot to the rest of the Benelux fleet is expected after a full winter of data has been collected, because cold weather performance is the main open question.

## Safety

Lost-time incidents fell to 7 in 2025, down from 12 in 2024, the lowest figure since the company started reporting them. The improvement is attributed to two changes. First, forklift training is now mandatory every 18 months for every operator rather than only at hiring, and refresher sessions include a practical assessment rather than a classroom test alone. Second, the company introduced a near-miss reporting app called SafeLoop, which lets warehouse staff report a hazard from a phone in under a minute, with a photo, and routes it to the site safety lead. More than 2,400 near misses were reported through SafeLoop in its first year, and site leads closed 91% of them within a week. The safety committee has noted that reports from the night shift are still much lower than from the day shift, which it believes reflects under-reporting rather than a genuinely safer shift, and it will run a targeted campaign on night shifts in early 2026.

## Technology

The most important technology project of the year is the migration of the warehouse management system from Manhattan to Blue Yonder. The migration was originally planned for the second quarter of 2025 but was delayed to the fourth quarter because mapping the data from the old system took far longer than expected: product master data, location codes and customer-specific handling rules had drifted apart across sites over a decade and had to be reconciled by hand before they could be loaded. The total cost of the migration is estimated at 2.3 million EUR, about 15% over the original budget, with the overrun almost entirely due to the extra data work and the cost of running both systems in parallel for longer. The programme is sponsored by the CTO, Amara Okafor, who has insisted that each site goes live only after a full peak-week simulation. Rotterdam and Lyon went live in November; the remaining sites are scheduled for the first quarter of 2026, outside the peak season.

## Customers

The largest customer is Helvetia Pharma, which accounted for 14% of revenue in 2025 under a five-year contract signed in 2024 that covers storage and distribution of temperature-controlled medicines. The board is aware that this concentration is a risk and has asked the commercial team to grow the rest of the pharmaceutical portfolio so that no single customer exceeds 12% of revenue by 2027. Customer churn, measured as the share of revenue from customers who left during the year, was 4.1%, an improvement on 5.3% in 2024. The annual customer survey produced a Net Promoter Score of 41, up from 36, with customers praising delivery reliability and criticising the speed of responses to billing queries, which the finance team has committed to fixing in 2026.

## Risks

The biggest operational risk is the driver shortage: at year end 9% of driver positions were vacant, concentrated in the Netherlands and northern France, and the company relied on agency drivers to cover the gap at a significantly higher cost. Insurance premiums rose by 17% at the January renewal, reflecting market conditions across the logistics sector rather than the company's own claims history, which improved. In February 2025 the company was targeted by a phishing attack in which an employee's email account was compromised; the attack was detected within hours, the account was locked, and the forensic investigation confirmed that no customer or personal data was lost. Since then, multi-factor authentication has been enforced for every account, including warehouse terminals that previously used shared logins.

## Plans for 2026

The board has approved a capital expenditure budget of 6.5 million EUR for 2026. The largest single item is a new hub in Milan, scheduled to open in the third quarter of 2026, which will serve both Italian retail customers and the growing pharmaceutical volume in northern Italy. The company has also set a target that 30% of last-mile deliveries will be made by electric vehicles by the end of 2026, which will require roughly forty more electric vans and charging infrastructure at three sites. The remaining budget is reserved for completing the warehouse software migration, replacing ageing forklifts in Poznań, and expanding the SafeLoop programme to drivers as well as warehouse staff.
